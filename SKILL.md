# IP Lead Scout

Find organizations with a recent, specific reason to consider IP services. Deliver a prospect list grounded in public evidence; a signal is an opening for research or outreach, not proof of budget, need, or buying intent.

## Scope the search

Use the user's geography, time window, service offering, and lead count when given. The default lookback window is six months from the current date; state the exact window used. Choose practical defaults for other missing parameters and state them in the result. Search all three segments unless the user narrows the request. If an in-scope segment yields fewer than 2 qualified leads after a reasonable search effort, report the gap explicitly and explain what search terms were tried. Do not fill the quota with weak matches.

## Qualifying signals

1. **Universities and research institutions:** Recent patent applications or grants, invention disclosures, licensing or spinout announcements, technology transfer programs, new commercialization funds, or partnerships involving identifiable research. Verify the institution's identity and its role; a patent listing alone does not establish a licensing need.
2. **Financial and insurance companies:** A documented product launch, new brand or digital service, entry into another jurisdiction, cross-border partnership, or expansion of an innovation program. Explain the plausible IP work tied to the specific development, such as trademark clearance, brand protection, licensing, or patent landscape work. Do not assume every product announcement creates a patent opportunity.
3. **General law firms building IP practices:** An announced new IP practice launch, IP partner/team hire, or IP-focused office expansion. Confirm the firm is a general law firm without an established IP practice; if it already has one, treat it as a competitor or referral partner, not a lead. Current IP-role recruitment at a firm without an established IP practice also counts as a signal.

Prioritize signals suggesting an upcoming decision, such as a proposed transfer under public notice, an IP-role opening, or a new technology transfer program. A pending transfer notice is not proof that the institution still needs an outside provider. Classify completed transfers and licenses as **context**, not sales leads: they show conversion activity but do not establish an open service opportunity. A patent grant alone is also context unless paired with a current commercialization step.

## Research and verification

- Search current public sources. Prefer the organization's dated announcement, a patent office or university technology-transfer record, a regulator filing, or the firm's own practice and hiring pages. Use reputable reporting to discover candidates, then locate the underlying source where possible.
- Open the page behind every candidate. Record the **specific observed event**, its event date, the source's publication date, the date you verified it, and a direct public URL to the supporting page or record. If a date is not stated, mark it as unknown rather than substituting another date. A search-result snippet, generic homepage, or URL that does not substantiate the event is insufficient.
- Distinguish application, publication, and grant dates for patent signals. Resolve name collisions and subsidiaries before attributing activity to an institution or company. For cross-border signals, name the jurisdictions actually supported by the source.
- For proposed transfers, compare the notice closing date with the verification date and note whether a transferee is already named. If the notice has closed, treat it as context or a status-check item unless newer evidence shows a decision is still pending; do not call it an active opportunity solely because the old page says “proposed.”
- Separate verified facts from an **IP-service hypothesis**. Keep the hypothesis specific to the event, label it as an inference, and note a material uncertainty if the fit is weak. Do not infer spend, existing counsel, procurement timing, or decision makers without evidence.
- For each lead, suggest one concrete **next action** that tests whether an opportunity remains, such as asking the technology transfer office whether outside agreement support is needed or monitoring a notice through its closing date. Recommend outreach only; do not contact anyone unless the user asks.
- Deduplicate organizations and repeated coverage of the same event. Prefer the newest meaningful signal; add a second URL only when it substantiates a distinct, relevant fact. Exclude leads with inaccessible evidence or only vague, undated claims.

## Deliverable

Present qualified leads in a compact table with: organization; buyer segment; specific signal and decision stage; event date; source date; verification date; direct public evidence URL; plausible IP-service angle (labeled **inference**); and next action. Put completed transactions in a separate **background context** section; do not count them toward a requested lead total. Include the search geography and lookback window. Make each URL clickable. Rank leads by recency, signal strength, and fit to the user's service offering, while keeping evidence and inference visibly separate.

Add a **Coverage note** for each of the three segments: number of qualified leads found and search terms used. If no leads were found, say whether the segment was searched without finding a qualifying public signal or was outside the user's requested scope and not searched. Do not present an unsearched segment as a market with no leads.

If asked for outreach copy or contact details, handle that as a follow-on request rather than inventing names or addresses.
